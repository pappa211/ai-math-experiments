# Independent actual-parent J2 classification review

Status: ACCEPTED ORDINARY for v2, at the supplied connected-support, single-edge-resilient low-gain interface. Arbitrary background and vertex identifications remain in H. Version 1 had an unnecessary fully-supported-H hypothesis and was not accepted for the actual-parent application; v2 explicitly corrects that scope. No computation, minimum-Q theorem, or Lean verification is used.

The complete one-base footprint assertion is valid. Two old spokes at the same endpoint inside an old clique would force an old triangle through their opposite endpoint and the clique edge between their apices, merging that endpoint into the clique despite the missing restored base. Spokes at opposite endpoints directly force the missing base. Thus one minimum cover per old clique can hit the entire supported ordinary-page family, not merely a selected page. This justifies both bare-page deductions.

For the matching cross graph bd,ce, the core pair abd,ace excludes every outside page on bc or de by an actual m+3 packing. A K4 family would supply such a page, so both families are books. Crossed book bases immediately give a two-edge new cover. In the only remaining orientation, cover saving forces bare ordinary pages abx and ady. Both apices are outside the core. If they agree, bdx is an old triangle through bare spokes, impossible. If they differ, abx,ady,ace are edge-disjoint; the only supported old footprint is ce, avoidable by an old maximum. This excludes the matching case without single-edge resilience.

For adjacent bd,be, the actual bde page forces the U-family to be a de-book. I checked the key three-page calculation for acx or bcx together with abd and dey, including equal outside apices. The whole de-family footprint bounds each old clique to one of bd,be,dy,ey. No acx spoke can coexist there with the displayed de-footprint, because a required core edge is absent. For bcx the only allowed coexistence is bx with bd, an adjacent pair; coexistence of bx with dy or ey would create a second de-family footprint in the same clique. The full old maximum therefore avoids every footprint and the triple appends. This excludes a T book: if no outside U-page exists, deleting its base instead leaves only the four-face K4 on abde and loses a packing unit. Hence the new triangles are exactly the two K4s abcx and abde. The absence of dx,ex follows from the prohibited ad/ae ordinary pages.

The cover arguments check separately. Supported cx plus ab,de saves one. Two supported ax,bx belong to distinct old components, so their simultaneous old-cover inclusion plus ab,de also saves one. A supported edge from ax,bx and one from bd,be likewise lie in distinct old components; their respective opposite edges complete covers of the two new K4s with only two additional edges. Therefore the attachment sets have exactly the listed possibilities, with at most one first-arm edge and no simultaneous first/second-arm attachment.

Connected support forces every old component to contain a selected attachment edge. Each such old clique meets the new core in exactly that edge: every other potential core vertex forces an explicitly absent old edge. The only possible pair of old cliques attaches on bd,be; they already share b and cannot share another vertex without merging across the absent de edge. This proves freshness and rules out hidden old identifications. Completeness of the actual triangle list identifies the support as the union of the two new K4s and these old leaves; it does not delete any background edges. Choosing the indicated central K4 gives precisely star_4, star_44, star_45, star_444, star_445, or star_455, with the new K4 always one leaf.

These named supports are admissible intermediate controls. Their actual-parent exclusion requires the separately accepted strict restoration theorem with arbitrary background. Together with the separately reviewed J0/J1 and J3/J4 cases, this completes the supplied low-gain shared-vertex interface; it is not a classification of arbitrary defect-one graphs.
