# Independent review: adjacent-blocker tight components are genuine triangle leaves

**PASS — PROVED_ORDINARY for the geometry and freshness statement.** The final no-tight-component consequence remains conditional on independent acceptance of the degree-three exclusions and the K5 triangle-leaf theorem. Reviewed `notes/adjacent_blocker_tight_pieces_v1.md`, SHA-256 `125b22d49cbd0047f70f52e52f97fb959907b637b16ba0b822b50f500a2c8d8d`. This is an independent review of the coordinator's boundary argument. It does not independently certify this reviewer's separately authored K5-leaf theorem.

For each literal old tight component D, the complete boundary has at most one spoke from the entire ordinary-page family of each restored base and possibly the old closing edge bc. Thus |F|<=3. The author correctly includes the necessary reason that the opposite-pair K4 exception cannot occur at size two: adjacent-base ordinary footprints are incident, while any footprint paired with bc shares b or c. The accepted <=2-boundary theorem therefore applies without silently excluding its exception.

At size three, bc and both ordinary footprints are present. Since bc lies in D, a cannot. Writing the footprints bx and cy, x!=y would make the actual old triangle axy share xy with D, forcing a into the old component despite missing ab/ac. Hence x=y and F is exactly the triangle {bc,bx,cx}. Every tight component would contain bc, so there is at most one. This reasoning uses all actual crossing triangles, not a selected boundary subset.

Universality of the named active support C makes D intersect C in at most one vertex: an edge of D between two C vertices would belong to an old triangle through C's universal vertex, merging the components. If the intersection were empty or private relative to {b,c,x}, then b,c,x all lie outside C. Triangle connectivity forces a new ordinary page, say aby, using a C edge ay; necessarily a,y are in C. Its apex cannot be in D, because that would add by to D's complete boundary. The known page acx is edge-disjoint from aby and has only the old supported footprint cx in D; the first page has only ay in C. There are no further old components. Maximum packings avoiding these individual edges combine with the two pages, contradicting the blocker pair's packing gain one. Thus no private D vertex is shared with C.

Finally a private D vertex z cannot belong to an outside old triangle, since C is the only other old component and z is not in C. Nor can it occur in a new triangle: it is not a restored terminal, and as an ordinary-page apex it would force an additional boundary edge bz or cz. Since Q is fully triangle-supported, this forbids every outside incident edge at z. Thus the K4 private vertex genuinely has degree three, and the K5 private pair genuinely has degree four and no neighbors beyond the boundary triangle and each other. This establishes the required vertex-region hypotheses; they are not inferred merely from the three-edge triangle boundary.

The K4/K5 exclusion dependencies are stated honestly. Once they are independently accepted, adjacent blocker restoration has no tight component. The remaining support is one C12 active component with retained background, and further vertex/background confinement is a separate step. The no-blocker branch and universal availability remain open.
