# Independent two-edge critical-envelope review

Status: ACCEPTED ORDINARY as a bounded actual-domain theorem on the stated exact empty-link fragile branch. It is not an exclusion, a completed paid reduction, an availability theorem, or authorization for enumeration. No numerical or Lean check.

Packing gain two over B forces every maximum to use two ordinary restored-base pages and a full old packing. A mixed two-base triangle cannot occur in that maximum, although it can exist elsewhere and must remain in the graph. The fixed pages have at most four supported old spokes, hence touch at most four old components. A critical edge outside those pages must be forced in every full local packing of a touched clique avoiding its page footprints. Arbitrary vertex sharing causes no edge conflict between old components.

All local forced sets were reconstructed directly. In K4, one forbidden edge forces its opposite; adjacent forbidden edges leave the opposite face as the only triangle; opposite forbidden edges leave no triangle and cannot occur in the fixed full old packing. In K5 with two adjacent forbidden edges ab,ac, any two-packing must use a and therefore ade, followed by bcd or bce; the forced set is exactly ade plus bc. With opposite forbidden edges ab,cd, the shared vertex of two packed triangles must have available degree four, so it is the fifth vertex e; the two displayed pairings force exactly its four spokes. For zero or one forbidden edge the stated pair-avoidance packings show no forced edge. These are exact local intersections, only an UPPER envelope for global criticality.

Distributing at most four footprint edges gives at most eight further forced edges, hence at most fourteen critical edges including the six page edges. Counting new vertices relative to the footprint endpoints gives at most two per footprint and at most six page vertices, hence fourteen incident vertices. Identifications can reduce these counts but do not imply isolated opposite edges or a two-hub form.

For an untouched old component every edge is resilient in W by local one-edge avoidance with the fixed pages retained. Thus an actual endpoint page can meet it only on the exceptional rim xy. Its complete additional boundary has one footprint per ordinary restored-base family AND, when the bases are adjacent, the unique possible mixed closing edge. That last term is necessary and correctly retained. An untouched component containing neither exception has boundary at most two and is removed by the full accepted boundary theorem. Each exceptional supported edge belongs to at most one old component, giving at most six pieces total, or five for disjoint bases.

The vertex confinement is valid for the minimum-Q support vertex set. A vertex outside the fixed pages, all retained B pieces, and x,y,u,v is incident with no critical W edge. Q support prevents it from neighboring either marked endpoint, since such a triangle would give a critical rim through it. It has no old B triangle and is not a restored-base endpoint. Every incident supported edge must therefore be on an ordinary r/f-page through it, restricting all its neighbors to the at most four base endpoints. Minimum degree five excludes it. This counts actual outside/background vertices, not merely old clique vertices.

The numerical bound 6+12+10+4=32 is conservative and correct: fixed pages, at most three additional vertices for each touched K5, at most five for each exceptional K5, and the four marked vertices. No claim that all formal identifications or edge assignments are realizable follows. Every actual old triangle must still satisfy B, and all nonextendable and mixed triangles remain constraints.

The result narrows the exact-defect-two fragile branch but does not close it, produce that branch, or address larger-defect W and arbitrary-codegree availability. Its value depends on a subsequent direct critical-envelope packing/cover argument; an unrequested census would not supply the missing global step.
