# Independent arbitrary-book coupled-release compression review

Status: ACCEPTED ORDINARY as a conditional physical packing operation. No availability, graph-order bound, solver replay, or Lean verification is claimed. Bound source: notes/arbitrary_book_release_compression_v1.md SHA-256 019b234fb7bac2ee1637847839986d0311c857d045ef0d3a69425cb8dd5e2659.

The coupled fan definition retains the necessary physical constraints. Each old endpoint fan avoids the reserved spoke because the reserved apex is one of the two deleted pages. Thus it is feasible and supplies f(empty)>=r. Any improvement at empty release, followed by the reserved uv-page, gives k+1 actual triangles, so f(empty)=r under the target packing equation. The same size calculation validates the displayed profitability threshold for a general release set.

A selected fan triangle can meet an outside packed triangle only in its physical rim, since outside triangles have neither endpoint. Each new rim has a unique label. Each label supplies at most one rim in each fan because those rims form a matching; the same physical rim is explicitly forbidden in both fans. Therefore a label not in D supplies zero or one selected triangle. Deleting these selected triangles restores disjointness from every label in S-D, and can lose at most |S-D| fan triangles. The remaining coupled fans preserve all intra-fan and inter-fan edge-disjointness, not just their separate matching sizes. This proves f(D)>=r+|D|+1. D cannot be empty by the exact empty-release bound.

For a doubly supplying label the two distinct triangle edges meet in a common neighbor g of u,v, different from the reserved apex h. Different labels cannot use the same g because even the u-fan alone is a rim matching. Hence D injects into X-{h}, giving |D|<=|X|-1. Neither submodularity nor addition of separate matching ranks is used. The proof works for arbitrary common-rim collisions precisely because the selected fans are coupled and remain a subpacking after compression.

The three-page control was checked ordinarily from its complete edge list. Its actual triangles are exactly the ten displayed. With both outside labels retained, the only feasible fan triangle is vb1b2. A singleton release adds one u rim and a competing v rim meeting the old v rim, so its optimum is two and it is not profitable. Releasing both labels permits all four u/v fan triangles with no physical rim collision, giving a profitable pair. This is the precise failure of singleton compression at the operation level.

The stated maximum-packing bounds are also correct. In L, T1 is a separate triangle component, and the remaining four triangles permit at most two; the displayed old packing has size three. In Q, at most one uv-page can be packed. Without those pages the two local triples contribute at most two each; if vb1b2 is selected, both v-fan triangles are excluded, giving at most three. Hence the maximum is at most five, attained by the displayed four fans plus uvh. Thus nu(L)=3 and nu(Q)=5, deliberately violating the required target equation nu(L)=nu(Q)-1. This is not a full-profile counterexample or an available augmentation inside the unknown Q.

Scope: a profitable arbitrary-codegree fan-only release, if available, reduces to at most codegree(uv)-1 outside labels, all used by both fans. The bound is local in codegree, not constant or a graph-size bound. It does not identify those labels, ensure a profit, or pay changes to the retained outside packing. At codegree two the compression reduces to the previously accepted singleton operation.
