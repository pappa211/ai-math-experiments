## B-plus-two classification

The path placement f=yt is correctly excluded. A maximum avoiding xy supplies an external e-page and the central f-page yzt. Any bare f-page with another apex is disjoint from that external e-page and appends over old maxima, contrary to yz-criticality, so zt is background. The residual packing condition supplies a noncentral f-page. A noncentral bare e-page must intersect every such f-page; with the disjoint bases this forces its apex t and all noncentral f-apices x. The resulting whole-base old cover plus zt saves one. If instead the only bare e-page is xyz, all three old path edges xy,yz,zt are background.

In the latter case the simultaneous footprint cover is valid. A clique with both footprints contains x,t. For distinct adjacent footprint edges xw,tq, the three possible incidences w=q, w=t, q=x each produce an actual old triangle through one of the background edges. These exhaust adjacency. Equal footprints require only one selected edge; distinct nonadjacent footprints form an opposite pair, contained in a minimum K4 cover. A minimum K5 cover contains any prescribed pair. Adding yz hits both bare pages and gives the forbidden cover saving. This is a cover argument, not an invalid packing-only exclusion of a three-edge path.

For f=zt, both noncentral apex families are nonempty by the two stated packing-resilience conditions. Distinct apices give disjoint pages avoiding yz. The only possible old packing obstruction is an opposite pair in K4. Such a K4 would contain x,p,t,q, while the old triangle xyt shares xt and forces the distinct fifth vertex y into its component. It is therefore impossible. The two families are one common singleton w. Background yz rules out yw; old triangles xyt,xwt then rule out xt. Finally a minimum old cover through supported zw, plus yz, would save one, so zw is background. Thus the four displayed wheel pages are the complete new triangle family, with no mixed-base page.

The claimed old-clique intersection bound follows from the missing diagonals and restored/background spokes. It allows old pieces on opposite rim edges to share vertices locally; the classification itself correctly does not assume freshness. The attached K4 control is consistent: the central four-wheel requires two opposite spokes in every minimum two-cover, so a rim edge cannot achieve a one-edge cover overlap.

## Actual-Q restoration

The actual cap hypotheses make u,v,c distinct from the wheel vertices. Every old piece without a rim edge has complete Q boundary at most two adjacent edges. This includes the possible v,z incidence: both marked-base footprints are vz. Triangles with multiple restored book edges and all c-pages add no unlisted old boundary. The accepted paid boundary theorem removes these pieces.

Rim-piece private vertices are fresh in actual A: adjacent rim pieces already share their rim endpoint, and a shared private vertex between opposite pieces creates an old triangle across an intervening rim edge, merging the components. For a private vertex a, triangle support and the exact new-page list imply that extra Q neighbors are only u,v. A purported extra old neighbor x or y outside its piece creates an old triangle through an intervening rim edge. Codegree two permits at most one endpoint neighbor. An endpoint outside a piece has at most one old neighbor in it, or it would already lie in that old clique component. The same new-page count bounds an otherwise unsupported vertex by degree three.

K4 on xy forces its two private vertices to attach to different endpoints. One is an additional cap: its endpoint edge is triangle-free in L and its two non-spine cap-spoke families have the same sole apex, the other private vertex. The accepted two-distinct-cap contradiction applies. K4 on yt or wx has complete boundary within the endpoint star; a minimum Q cover through the private opposite face can replace its three edges by a two-edge clique cover, saving one. K4 on tw either has small boundary or contains an endpoint, leaving its other private vertex of degree three.

For K5 on xy,yt,wx, at most two of three private vertices gain an endpoint neighbor; an untouched private vertex is genuinely degree four and simplicial. This is excluded by the independently established general simplicial theorem. For K5 on tw with an endpoint inside, the other two private vertices cannot have the opposite endpoint as a neighbor; complete boundary is the indicated triangle and they are the genuine fresh vertices of the accepted K5 triangle-leaf theorem. Thus all old pieces are removed.

With only the central six-vertex L support left, the displayed cover and packing prove nu=tau=3; deleting xy leaves the five-rim wheel of cover number three. Hence xy is ineligible, contradicting the surviving cap branch. This closes the degree-four cap descent branch. Its simplicial dependency is acyclic: that theorem was accepted independently and used no wheel-rim/cap reconstruction. One could now also invoke the later minimum-degree-five consequence, but this review binds the original weaker acyclic dependency.
