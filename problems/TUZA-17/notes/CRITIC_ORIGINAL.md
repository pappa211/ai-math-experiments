## 1. Original objections and resolutions

### A-O1: the blow-up colour bound might silently assume monochromatic clone classes

Resolved. For arbitrary clone colour counts, independent uniform representatives give expected base monochromatic-edge count equal to the clone count divided by t². Every representative outcome has at least m_r(B) monochromatic edges. The reverse bound follows by a uniform lift. This establishes exact t²m_r(B) without a uniformity assumption. For B=B0 Cartesian K2, each layer costs at least m_r(B0), and a derangement of all r labels on the second layer simultaneously keeps that cost and avoids monochromatic identity edges. The hypotheses r≥2 and connected triangle-free B0 with chi(B0)>r are adequate.

### A-O2: the prescribed-packing extension might consume a future prescribed edge or unbalance a backbone pair

Resolved after checking the full construction. At a given apex, previous rim perfect matchings contribute forbidden degree at most q−1 and later prescribed edges contribute at most two, hence Δ(F)≤q+1. The current prescribed edges avoid F by induction. Mirrored companions avoid all prescribed endpoints and previous companions, so they cannot introduce a matching conflict. At most six excluded clone positions per class and q+1 forbidden second endpoints are dominated by t≥2q+8. Each prescribed within-layer edge and its companion remove equal numbers from both sides of each affected identity pair; an identity edge does the same directly. With at most two prescribed edges, no pair loses more than two positions on either side. Thus n≥t−2≥2(q+1), and the supplied Hall proof gives a completion avoiding F. Preserving future prescribed EDGES is sufficient: different apex matchings may legitimately share rim vertices.

The proof covers zero, one and two prescribed triangles, including different-apex triangles sharing a rim vertex. It does not claim extension for three prescribed triangles. The finite checks below supplement, rather than prove, this universally quantified construction.

### A-O3: the strengthened examples might be mistaken for actual critical graphs

Removing any spoke lowers nu by exactly one, whereas true criticality preserves nu under deletion of any one or two edges. The explicit cover of size q(b−1)t also gives positive Tuza slack. The examples refute the specified colour repair even after adding fixed codegree/connectivity thresholds, shared-edge triangle connectivity and one/two-triangle extension; they do not refute a claim using the full critical package. The q-apex connectivity proof correctly leaves at least one apex and one rim vertex after fewer than q vertex deletions when bt≥q.

### A-O4: triangle adhesion might discard crossing triangles or use a cover that need not meet the boundary

Resolved at the stated common-triangle scope. The lemma explicitly assumes every actual triangle lies in one piece. Under global packing K+1, gains are at most one. Singleton suppliers in two different pieces would give gain two; hence one piece supplies all three edges and other pieces have zero two-edge gains. The dominant piece's cover meets the boundary because the boundary is an actual triangle. This last step fails for three arbitrary nontriangle boundary edges; the notes correctly retain that gap. The 4-connectivity corollary is therefore a reconstructed known consequence, not a novel global separator theorem. I did not independently audit the entire Freitas thesis or its priority history.

### B-O2: a good arbitrary H-cover might be substituted for a minimum H-cover

Resolved where positive AC is claimed, and retained as an unresolved branch elsewhere. The exact cover identity ranges over ALL H covers. AC requires a cover of cardinality exactly tau(H). The saturation construction has size 2k+m=tau(H) because every inequality in the squeeze is equality. AC-0 has size 2h=tau(H), AC-1e has size 2(h−1)+1=tau(H), and the frontier-essential construction has size m+1+2(k−1)=tau(H). Thus these are genuinely minimum covers.

For m=b−1 with tight J=H−M, the analogous block cover union M has size tau(H)+1. The frozen note correctly does NOT infer AC from it. Its separate Tuza and critical-subcase consequences are valid, but cannot be relabelled minimum-cover compatibility. This is the most important unresolved minimum-cover restriction in the first frontier.

### B-O3: the packing inequality or deletion type might be reversed

Resolved. Appending M's apex pages to an H−M packing gives |M|+nu(H−M)≤p, hence nu(H−M)≤p−|M|. Coupled with cover loss and Aharoni, this yields tau(H)−m≤tau(J)≤2nu(J)≤2p−2m and m≤b. At m=b every term is equal. At m=b−1, integrality forces nu(J)=p−m and tau(J) in {2k−1,2k}. All these deletions are matching EDGES, with endpoints retained.

### B-O4: outside incidence or block vertex sharing might defeat the chosen packings

Resolved. Blocks are edge-disjoint, not required vertex-disjoint. In AC-1e an e-triangle shares at most one edge with a J block, since two shared edges would force e into that complete block. Choosing avoiding faces gives the required h-packing. A triangle contains at most one induced-link edge because two would place all three vertices in X. This controls its possible intersection with M. In the frontier-essential proof, all surviving old-supported link edges share the unique endpoint of e in X; all other surviving link edges belong to no H triangle and can be appended as apex pages to EVERY maximum old packing. The star-plus-unsupported-edge cover bound is consequently valid. The D=0 branch is explicitly routed to AC-0.

### B-O5: small-budget deductions might use an unjustified triangle-free matching estimate

Resolved, including frozen addendum 1. For every edge of a maximum matching, one endpoint has no neighbor among unmatched vertices: two distinct such neighbors would augment, while one shared neighbor would make a triangle. The single safe endpoint gives vc≤2m−1 for m≥1. Choosing one safe endpoint per matching edge gives m vertices. For m≥3 they cannot all be pairwise adjacent in a triangle-free graph, so two can be removed from the matching endpoint cover, giving vc≤2m−2. This proves the b≤4 corollary with the stated small cases. A remaining counterexample must satisfy b≥5, m≥3, 2m−2>b and m≤b−1. These are necessary conditions only. Two disjoint C5s attain m=4, vc=6 as a link graph, but do not themselves constitute an AC instance or counterexample.

## 2. Later structural supplements

### Critical apex nonsaturation

Both the original Tutte–Berge argument and the elementary supplement are valid. In the former, a nonempty minimizing set gives a one-vertex blocker; an even component with empty minimizing set gives a one-vertex blocker; an odd edge-containing component gives a two-vertex blocker. The inequalities have the correct directions.

I checked the elementary supplement against [Schrijver's one-page CWI proof](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/tutteb.pdf). Its extremal choice ranges over maximum matchings and unmatched pairs. The replacement edge xy remains a matching edge after deleting y's old matching edge, still misses t, and increases the intersection with M: x is N-unmatched, t is N-unmatched, and the removed N-edge cannot be the M-edge xy. The equal-cardinality count supplies a choice x different from t. Thus the supplement really removes an unproved Tutte–Berge dependency from the final ordinary proof chain.

Deleting one/two blocker vertices of the LINK means deleting one/two SPOKES of G. Critical resilience preserves p, while the old/apex split gives p≤nu(G−v)+mu(L)−1. Appending any maximum link matching to an old packing avoiding its edges then gives nu((G−v)−M)≤nu(G−v)−1. This is a substantive graph-link consequence, rather than purely generic hypergraph arithmetic. It still supplies no cover-saving operation. K4 and K5 prevent nonsaturation alone from being presented as a counterexample characterization. The abstract Fano plane has not been assigned an actual graph apex/link representation.

### Tight matching deletion and link-component transfer

Frozen addendum 2 is valid for ANY matching M with tight J=H−M. A J block meeting X belongs to one component because it contains at most two X vertices and any two are adjacent. Each lost old triangle contains exactly one M edge; all its edges meet that component's X vertices. Therefore a lost page can share a block edge only with a block assigned to the same component. Pages from different components share no graph edge, even when their third outside vertex is identical. Distinct J blocks already have disjoint edges, and background blocks avoid X.

Adding all L_i edges to H_i creates no new graph edges, so every H_i triangle is already classified. Unsupported link edges remain in the correct component but contribute no old triangle. Irrelevant non-link edges in no triangle cannot help a minimum old cover. Consequently packing and cover numbers add over the complete edge-support partition. In G, different components also have distinct apex spokes. The union of minimum C_i and background opposite pairs is a minimum H cover, and vertex-cover numbers of the residual links add. This proves actual AC transfer, not merely an upper bound for a nonminimum cover.

The small-component corollary (matching number at most three per component), the two-C5 tight-deletion consequence, and the stated single-triangle-component obstruction for a genuine critical graph are valid. Shared outside vertices do not evade the separation. Connected links of larger matching number and slack-one stable J are not settled.
