### A: adversarial derivation

The Mycielski lower bound survives the recolouring test: in a purported k-colouring, every clone avoids the apex colour; recolouring originals of that colour by their clones' colours leaves all original edges proper. Adjacent originals could not both have the old apex colour. A triangle involving a clone projects to a base triangle, while triangles involving the new apex require a forbidden clone-clone edge. Iteration from K2 therefore gives the required triangle-free graph of chromatic number r+1 and no isolates. For r=4 the separately reconstructed C5-to-11-to-23 construction agrees with the certificate.

For any r-colouring of B[t], a random transversal has at least one monochromatic base edge. The expectation counts each physical monochromatic edge with probability t^-2, so this is a lower bound on actual deletion cost, uniformly over all partitions. It does not depend on an approximate maximum cut or a planted subset of edges. Cone triangles are exactly apex plus a rim edge because the rim has no triangles. The packing upper bound is therefore a bound for EVERY packing: each triangle uses two of bt spokes. With t>b, t^2>bt>=2nu. Adding a nonnegative residual cover cannot repair the inequality. The budget proof permits every possible global packing and every residual cover.

The cone itself satisfies Tuza independently: replace each selected rim edge in a cone cover by one endpoint's spoke (that rim edge appears in only its own apex triangle), and use matching versus vertex-cover accounting on the rim. Endpoints of any maximal matching give an actual cover of size twice that matching. This distinguishes mechanism refutation from a graph counterexample.

The four-colour residual claim is also valid: identify colours with F_2^2; colour a retained edge by the nonzero difference of its endpoint colours. In any retained triangle its three vertex colours are distinct, and the three edge differences are the three distinct nonzero vectors. This explicit argument is enough; no Aharoni theorem is needed for the negative result. K10/K11 balanced-cut and Cartesian control arithmetic is correct. Arbitrary overlapping K4/K5 vertex-gluings were not universally checked for A4; the frozen note retains that limit.

The apex state formulas quantify over all H-covers and all link matchings. Restricting the cover formula to minimum H-covers would be an extra theorem and is not silently allowed. Likewise an arbitrary prescribed maximum H-packing need not survive deleting a link matching. The stated formulas do not make either error.

### B: transfer directions and full boundary

Every destroyed triangle is met by B: a triangle containing X has exactly one such vertex (X is independent), and its other two vertices are in S; a destroyed triangle avoiding X uses a removed B edge. This handles outside common neighbors and triangles entirely inside S. At most q packing triangles can be destroyed, because an edge-disjoint family must consume distinct B edges. Conversely the coloured q-triangle family uses only removed edges and can join every residual packing. Thus both bounds on nu are valid.

For covers, restriction to retained edges covers R. The q edge-disjoint triangles outside R force at least q additional cover edges, giving the lower bound; B plus a residual cover gives the upper bound. These are independent certificates, not imported optimal slack. Substitution gives delta(R)=delta(G)+q with the correct sign. Removing nonempty X strictly decreases |V|+|E|, including q=0. None of this proves such X exists in an arbitrary counterexample.

The half-saturation inequality follows from tau(G)<=tau(R)+q and nu(G)>=nu(R)+p. Rearranging gives delta(R)>=delta(G)+2p-q. In the arbitrary-independent-set version, every apex triangle still has exactly one X vertex, and its base is deleted; hence the packing remains disjoint from R_X. The dense bound 2rho_X<=sum_x 2floor(d(x)/2)<=alpha(G)(n-1)<ell<=|B_X| is correct even when bases overlap, because overlap only restricts packings further. For K_n-M, alpha=2 and an incident vertex has link size binom(n-2,2)-(t-1). The lower estimate is strictly above 2(n-1) at n=9 and its successive difference is at least n-5 thereafter.
