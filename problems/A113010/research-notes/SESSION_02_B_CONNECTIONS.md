# Session 02 — B: connections, exact diagnostics, and rejected routes

Prepared 2026-10-27, bounded research pass. This note proves two structural facts about the barrier bases `k=10^T+1`. Neither proves the A113010 classification or the barrier family's required inequality. No new global cutoff is claimed.

## 1. Strongest finding: unrestricted minimum digit weight is exactly two

**Proposition.** Let `T>=1`, `k=10^T+1`, and `s>=1`. Then

```
ord_(k^s)(10) = 2*T*k^(s-1).
```
The minimum decimal digit sum among all positive multiples of `k^s` is exactly two. The smallest positive multiple having digit sum two is

```
10^(T*k^(s-1)) + 1,
```
and its decimal length is exactly `T*k^(s-1)+1`.

**Proof of the order formula.** Put `B=10^T`. The order of 10 modulo `k=B+1` divides `2T`. If it were a proper divisor `d` of `2T`, then `d<=T`, whereas `0<10^d-1<k`; that is impossible. Thus this order is exactly `2T`.

Any exponent yielding 1 modulo `k^s` must therefore be `2T*h`. Every prime `p|k` is odd, and `gcd(B-1,B+1)=1` because both are odd and their difference is two. Consequently

```
v_p(10^(2T)-1) = v_p(k).
```
The elementary lifting-the-exponent identity for odd primes gives

```
v_p(10^(2T*h)-1) = v_p(k)+v_p(h).
```
Hence `k^s | 10^(2T*h)-1` if and only if `k^(s-1)|h`, proving the formula without assuming that k is prime or squarefree.

Put `H=T*k^(s-1)`. Since `k^(s-1)` is odd, the plus-sign version of the same valuation identity gives, for every `p|k`,

```
v_p(10^H+1) = v_p(k)+v_p(k^(s-1)) = s*v_p(k).
```
Thus `10^H == -1 (mod k^s)`. Inside the cyclic subgroup generated by 10, its order is `2H`, and the exponents giving `-1` are precisely `H (mod 2H)`.

A number of decimal digit sum one is a power of ten and cannot be divisible by k. A number of digit sum two is either `2*10^a`, which also cannot be divisible by `k^s>=11`, or `10^a+10^b` with `a>b>=0`. In the latter case divisibility is equivalent to `10^(a-b)==-1 (mod k^s)`, so `a-b>=H`. Its least possible value is attained at `b=0,a=H`. This proves every assertion.

**Immediate length consequence.** For `s>=2`, the shortest weight-two multiple has length at least `k+1`. Therefore no positive multiple of `k^s` with at most k decimal digits has digit sum two. This is a genuine length-sensitive exclusion, although it treats only fixed weight two.

**Why this connection matters.** The relation `k^s | sum_i 10^ei` is an additive zero-sum/shortest-word problem modulo a growing prime-power product. Ordinary minimum weight is completely uninformative on the exact barrier family: it is always two, while the short interval relevant to the problem contains no such weight-two word. Any successful coding or automaton theorem must jointly control weight and span.

This is stronger diagnostic evidence than saying fixed moduli admit compatible strings. It uses the full modulus `k^s` and gives its exact first minimum-weight witness. It remains a diagnostic, not a promising general proof by itself.

## 2. A sharp specialization-to-polynomial-identity lemma

**Proposition.** Let `T>=1`, `k=10^T+1`, and `P(X)` be a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. If

```
P(1)<=10  and  k | P(10),
```
then `X^T+1` divides `P(X)` in `Z[X]`. In particular `P(1)` is even.

**Proof.** Reduce monomials by `X^T=-1`, obtaining the remainder

```
R(X)=sum_(r=0)^(T-1) [sum_(q>=0) (-1)^q a_(qT+r)] X^r.
```
Its coefficient l1 norm is at most `P(1)<=10`. Also `R(10)==P(10)==0 (mod k)`, while

```
|R(10)| <= 10*10^(T-1) = 10^T < k.
```
Therefore `R(10)=0`. A nonzero integer polynomial of coefficient l1 norm at most ten cannot vanish at ten: if its leading term is `cX^r`, `r>=1`, its lower terms have absolute value at ten at most `(10-|c|)*10^(r-1) < |c|*10^r`; the constant case is immediate. Hence `R=0`. Finally, evaluation at one gives `P(1)=2Q(1)` for the integer quotient Q.

The coefficient bound is sharp, including among decimal digit polynomials. Set

```
P(X)=X^(2T)+X^(T+1)+9.
```
Its weight is 11 and

```
P(10)=(10^T+1)(10^T+9),
```
but it is not divisible by `X^T+1`. For `T>=2`, its remainder is `10-X`, a nonzero polynomial that vanishes at ten. For `T=1`, the polynomial is `2X^2+9`, whose remainder modulo `X+1` is 11.

**Logical role and novelty.** This does rigorously pass from numerical divisibility to polynomial divisibility, something general sparse-polynomial/root-multiplicity arguments cannot assume. It excludes odd weights 1,3,5,7,9 for all multiples of these bases. However, the exact failure at weight eleven prevents scaling this lemma to the required weight `s>=2501` without genuinely new carry control. Applying a polynomial root-multiplicity theorem after merely assuming numerical divisibility to exponent s would still be invalid.

**Minimum test for an extension.** Any proposal extending the numerical-to-polynomial implication beyond weight ten must account explicitly for `10-X` and the family above. A proposed higher-multiplicity inference must additionally explain why `k^s|P(10)` forces more than one polynomial factor; the proposition does not do this.

## 3. Automata/sturdiness: a precise connection, but no useful complexity reduction

Clokie, Lidbetter, Molina Lovett, Shallit and Witzman study the smallest digit sum of a positive multiple and give shortest-path/finite-automaton algorithms. Their conference paper's Section 7, Theorem 15, gives a linear-in-modulus binary sturdiness test. [Primary paper, FUN 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2020/12771/pdf/LIPIcs-FUN-2021-10.pdf). The expanded article [Computational aspects of sturdy and flimsy numbers, TCS 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2022.05.029) describes the extension to arbitrary display base.

For a modulus M, use residue states r and digit transitions `r -> (10r+a) mod M`, edge weight a, and a separate leading-nonzero start. A path of at most L digits ending at zero represents a positive multiple of M of length at most L; its weight is the decimal digit sum. Layering this graph by length is exact. Here `M=k^s` and `L=k`, so ordinary construction has exponentially many states relative to k. No compressed symbolic certificate was found.

More fundamentally, on the length curve `10^(k-1)<=k^s<10^k`, every positive multiple of `k^s` of length at most k is `j*k^s` for `1<=j<=9`. Thus a minimum-weight bound greater than s is a stronger statement about at most nine specific large powers/multiples, not an automatic simplification of the original problem. The proposition in Section 1 rejects dropping length completely.

A potentially useful result would be an explicit, uniform weight-versus-span theorem for these growing moduli. None was found. Merely naming the required minimum-distance inequality supplies no new mechanism, and it should not be promoted as a solution route.

## 4. Generalized abc: coefficient and quantifier obstruction

The Browkin–Brzezinski n-conjecture concerns nonzero integer tuples summing to zero, having total gcd one and no proper zero subsum. Its predicted limiting quality is `2n-5`, not one or `n-2`. Pairwise-coprime variants have different hypotheses and cannot be silently substituted. These exact statements are recorded as Conjectures 1.2, 1.4 and 1.8 in [Hölzl–Kleine–Stephan, 2025, pp. 62–64](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/881532D8B69924BBFA5C76A32A52063A/S1446788725000084a.pdf/improved_lower_bounds_for_strong_nconjectures.pdf).

For the coprime-to-ten case, split each decimal coefficient into unit terms to obtain

```
10^e1 + ... + 10^es - k^s = 0.
```
When a units term occurs, this tuple has gcd one, and positivity ensures no proper zero subsum. Its radical is `rad(10k)<=10k`; its height is `k^s`. The conjectural n-term exponent is `2s-3` because `n=s+1`. Consequently even an ideal bound with a benign constant would only compare exponent s with approximately 2s, giving no contradiction. Retaining m nonzero digit coefficients gives a radical at most `210k` and the exponent `2m-3` (divide out the total gcd, at most nine, so the height remains at least `k^s/9`); it could only have a favorable coefficient when `m` is substantially below `s/2`. The equation `S=s` permits `m` as large as s. In addition, the conjectural constants depend on m, which grows.

Thus even assuming this major conjecture does not directly settle the problem by this substitution. The necessary improvement is both structural (exploit positive decimal terms more strongly than general n-term abc) and uniform in the dimension. No such strengthening was established or located.

## 5. Discriminating computation and disposition

A tempting block-carry assertion is that every base-B digit of `(B+1)^s` is nonzero whenever `s<=B/4`. It is false: for `B=35,s=7`, its base-35 digits, from low to high, are

```
[1, 7, 21, 0, 1, 22, 7, 1].
```
This is a tiny exact computation, not a scan of A113010. It was shared with the coordinator for the independent block-carry investigation. It does not refute a narrower theorem with B a power of ten.

No new plausible uniform route emerged from this pass. The useful deliverables are the complete order/first-weight-two-witness theorem, the sharp weight-ten polynomial-lifting lemma, and precise rejection of applying unrestricted sturdy-number distance or generic n-term abc.
