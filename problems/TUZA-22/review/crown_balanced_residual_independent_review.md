# Independent review: balanced crown residual and avoidance boundary

Verdict: ACCEPT as ORDINARY_PROOF of the supplied crown interface, with the stated simultaneous-footprint gap. Source `notes/crown_balanced_residual_interface_v1.md`, SHA256 ea97baf799db12c4c5e8d47eb3aac775b2adba0066d84b2b522b1342305994f5.

For any maximum independent set of q=floor(r/2) crown triangles, the selected triangles are pairwise edge-disjoint because all 2r named edges are distinct and adjacent crown triangles are the only pairs sharing an e-edge. Their support is deleted. Every lost noncrown triangle must use a selected f-edge, since each e-edge has exactly its two specified crown occurrences. Choosing those q f-edges covers every such loss and all selected crown triangles. If r is even, each unselected crown triangle is isolated in the cyclic index pattern; if odd, precisely one consecutive pair and q−1 isolated unselected triangles remain. Their q-edge transversal can be chosen among the deleted e-edges. Thus the 2q cover cost is valid in both parities and for every maximum independent crown choice.

The packing and cover inequalities force a tight B residual using proper-subgraph CS, with no omitted pages on surviving or deleted f-edges. For r=4 the deleted edges are exactly the union of two edge-disjoint actual triangles, so the previously accepted two-triangle restoration theorem excludes the configuration at minimum CS. For odd r there is an extra deleted e-edge, and for larger even r more triangles are restored; neither is silently covered by that existing theorem.

The literal footprint counterexample is correct. The square of C6 is the octahedral graph: it has six consecutive-vertex triangles plus the two parity triangles. Each cycle edge lies in just two consecutive triangles. A new vertex adjacent to one parity triple adds three triangles on that triple's distance-two edges without changing the cycle-edge codegrees. Deleting the opposite-parity crown packing deletes all cycle edges and its three distance-two bases, leaving the other parity triple and the new vertex as a literal K4. The surviving boundary bases are a triangle face, which meets every K4 triangle, so its maximum packing cannot avoid their simultaneous footprint. This is solely a failure of the proposed avoidance step; no exact global tightness is asserted.

No transfer of crown availability from cographic geometries to arbitrary graph triangle families is justified by this note. The cited primary-source attribution is retained, while the balanced reduction and geometric control were independently reconstructed here.
