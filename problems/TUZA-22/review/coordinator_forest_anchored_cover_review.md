The forest alternating-reach proof is valid with disconnected components and isolates: opposite-parity reach would augment a maximum matching, every reached odd vertex is matched to an even reached vertex, and the unreached set is perfectly matched internally. Only the direction that an even reached vertex can be exposed is needed. If the unreached set Z is nonempty, the forest edge count gives at most |Z|/2-1 edges after deleting its perfect matching, so its remaining cover slack is elementary and strict.

For an apex w, an exposable neighbor allows adding its spoke to a maximum forest matching and selecting w in Q. With no exposable neighbor, nonempty Z supplies one unit of cover slack to pay for w. If Z is empty, all w-neighbors lie in the odd reached set A, which itself covers the entire link and has size equal to the matching. The only case where M meets w explicitly includes w in Q. Empty and edgeless cases satisfy the same construction; no minimum degree or closed-link assumption has been imported.

At a selected forest leaf v, every rim edge away from its selected parent w is selected, since the other two edges of that actual triangle cannot be selected. Hence the FULL link minus w is a forest. Removing every v-edge and every selected matching rim edge removes at least the selected edge vw, so induction on selected-edge count terminates. Its residual selected set still covers all surviving triangles.

The appended hub triangles are disjoint from the entire residual graph. The loss cover selects every deleted matching rim edge directly, covering arbitrary outside pages on it; all other lost triangles contain v and are covered by the complete link tuple. Every new selected anchor is either a rim edge of M or the sole possible selected spoke vw. The pointed property pays for vw exactly when the new packing uses it. The old anchors remain in the residual cover, and the cost inequality is exactly twice the appended packing size. Thus the existential anchored theorem is self-contained and ordinary.

Global minimum-cover optimality bounds the component size by |D| plus that family optimum q. An outside page can meet an internal packing only on its base, and all such used selected bases belong to D. The joint packing therefore has weight2|I|+q at least the component's selected-edge count. No minimum internal cover, maximum internal packing, or arbitrary component availability is assumed.
