# Independent review: exact outside-page forest minmax

Verdict: ACCEPT as ORDINARY_PROOF, including arbitrary page subfamilies and without minimum-cover optimality. Frozen source `notes/challenger_forest_external_minmax_v1.md`, SHA256 926a9fd211d1fcbed389f8670bafeedc8dc3f9e84fbe2a773eb14292a7bce429.

I checked the bag separation directly from actual edges. A page on forest edge uv belongs exactly to endpoint bags u,v. Across a forest cut, two exclusive pages can conflict only if their bases share an endpoint and their apex agrees, which places both bases on the same side; the only crossing base is the separating edge itself, whose pages form the shared clique. Thus the bag decomposition is an actual clique sum with no hidden edges across the separator. Each bag is precisely the line graph of its base-versus-apex bipartite incidence graph, even for arbitrary missing pages. Every induced subgraph retains this description.

The bipartite edge-color proof is sufficient: balancing parts and filling degree deficits with parallel edges gives a regular bipartite multigraph, Hall yields a perfect matching, and iteration gives a decomposition into maximum-degree-many matchings. Every line-graph clique has a common endpoint. Clique gluing then preserves equality of coloring and clique numbers by permuting the separator colors, also in every induced subgraph.

The replication argument is correct at its delicate unchanged-clique-number branch. The duplicated vertex belongs to no maximum original clique. Every maximum clique therefore meets its original color class at a different vertex, so removing that color class except the duplicated vertex lowers clique number. Recoloring the remainder with one fewer color leaves the old nonneighbors plus the new twin as an independent final color. Induced graphs with both twins use the same argument; those with at most one inherit perfection.

For the clique-cover consequence, replicating according to membership in all maximum independent sets yields exactly k*alpha vertices with a known k-coloring and independence number at most alpha. Perfection supplies a k-clique. It intersects every displayed color class, and projection gives an original clique meeting every maximum independent set. Deleting that clique lowers alpha by exactly one, so induction covers the original graph by alpha cliques. Vertices in no maximum independent set, deleted only during replication, remain covered by the final induction on the original graph. No complement-perfection theorem has been assumed.

Finally, every intersection clique is an actual edge fiber. Its distinct forest bases are pairwise incident and hence share a tree vertex. Within that endpoint bag, the bipartite line-graph clique is a common base or a common apex, giving a shared base edge or spoke in the actual graph. Therefore graph-edge transversals and clique covers of the intersection graph have exactly the same optimum, while triangle packings are exactly its independent sets. This proves nu=tau for the specified outside family.

The only stated limitation is substantive: additional internal triangles are a different family and can conflict through outside-page bases. The theorem does not settle their joint weighted selection. No finite computation or deep unproved perfect-graph result is a premise of the supplied proof.
