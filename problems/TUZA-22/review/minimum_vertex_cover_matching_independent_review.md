# Independent review: specified minimum-cover saturation and star component

For the prescribed minimum vertex cover Q, its complement I is maximum independent. In the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition, call an exposable factor-critical component bad when disjoint from I. If a collection of bad components had fewer neighboring A vertices than components, deleting those neighbors from I and adding one vertex per component would enlarge I. Thus Hall matches all bad components. One can even restrict this initial matching to A∩I, but the producer's weaker version into all A is sufficient.

The preservation step is valid. In the A-versus-component bipartite incidence graph, a matching saturating A exists. Hence any smaller matching has an augmenting path. Its endpoints lie on opposite sides; choosing the unmatched A endpoint as the start gives an unmatched component at the end. Flipping retains the matched status of every component already covered, so bad components remain matched throughout. For each final attached component, delete the actual attachment endpoint and match the rest perfectly. Every unattached component intersects I, permitting an I vertex to be exposed. The remaining region has a perfect matching. The final matching has the Gallai–Edmonds maximum cardinality and exposes no Q vertex. No claim that Q must be matched into its complement is needed.

The star application also survives arbitrary attachments. All selected edges in an actual triangle belong to one selected-edge component, because they are pairwise incident. An edge-support union of triangles assigned to the star therefore contains no selected edge from any other component. Every actual triangle in that union is still assigned to the star, and so contains its center h. This proves that the union is literally a cone over a triangle-free rim, rather than merely a designated family with omitted crossing triangles.

The star leaves Q cover every rim edge. Replacing a chosen rim edge of a triangle transversal by either endpoint spoke is safe, because the rim is triangle-free and that edge lies in just its own cone triangle. Conversely a rim vertex cover gives a spoke transversal. If Q were not a minimum rim cover, its smaller spoke replacement together with the other selected components would improve the original ordinary cover. Hence the general saturation theorem applies. In a saturating rim matching, an edge inside Q accounts for two Q endpoints and an edge leaving Q accounts for one; there are no edges wholly outside Q. These are exactly the stated internal/external triangle weights, so the weighted total is m, not just a lower estimate.
