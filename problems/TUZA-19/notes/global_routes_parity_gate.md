# Odd triangle transversals: a global binary gate and an exact critical cone obstruction

Status: new auxiliary gate under investigation; criticality-only form REFUTED below. The refined criticality-plus-two-edge-resilience form remains UNRESOLVED. No unrestricted proof or Tuza counterexample is claimed. All graph triangles are actual, covers use graph edges, and packings are edge-disjoint.

## Definition and exact global implication

Let π(G) be the minimum |C| over C⊆E(G) such that every actual triangle T has |C∩E(T)| odd, hence1 or3. Such a C is an ordinary triangle cover, so τ(G)≤π(G).

**H-O2.** Every finite simple graph satisfying both

    τ(G−e)=τ(G)−1 for every edge e,
    ν(G−S)=ν(G) for every |S|≤2,

satisfies π(G)≤2ν(G).

This would prove unrestricted Tuza. Indeed an edge-minimal counterexample has k=ν(G), τ(G)=2k+1, and the two displayed properties: a deletion that lowered ν after at most two edges would give τ(G)≤2(k−1)+2=2k; one-edge minimality then forces τ(G−e)=2k. Thus τ≤π≤2ν would contradict its existence.

H-O2 is a stronger, falsifiable auxiliary claim on ordinary graphs satisfying the EXACT displayed assumptions. It does not require a hypothetical positive defect as an input. Its weakest link is the existence of a cheap odd transversal: an ordinary cheap cover may meet some triangles twice, and no parity-repair theorem has been supplied.

The edge-only form H-O1, obtained by dropping the second displayed condition, is false even in connected graphs with one shared-edge triangle component, and its gap is unbounded. The example below is deliberately separated from H-O2.

## Why this is different from a single vertex cut

Let A be the triangle-by-edge incidence matrix over F2. The all-ones edge vector solves Ax=1 because every triangle has three edges. Put

    Z_Δ={z∈F2^E : Az=0}.

Then π(G)=min_{z∈Z_Δ}|1+z|. Every vertex-cut vector belongs to Z_Δ, since a triangle crosses a cut in0 or2 edges, but Z_Δ may be larger than the cut space. Thus this optimization retains all triangle-parity constraints while allowing independent states around longer cycles not generated by triangles.

Unlike a single vertex-global cut, π adds over shared-edge triangle components: their edge sets and triangle constraints are disjoint, even if their vertex sets overlap. The K5 incidence obstruction from global_routes_critical_cut_obstruction.md therefore has π=4b=2ν: choose a minimum four-cover separately in each K5, each of which meets every face in1 or3 edges. That obstruction does not refute H-O2.

## Cone parity is exactly bipartization

For any rim graph R, let G add one apex a adjacent to every rim vertex. An odd transversal has spoke indicators x_v. The apex triangle auv forces the rim-edge indicator

    c_uv=1+x_u+x_v  (mod2).

These values automatically satisfy every old rim-triangle equation as well. Color a by0 and v by1+x_v. Then c_uv and x_v are exactly the indicators of monochromatic edges under this coloring. Conversely every monochromatic-cut edge set is odd on each triangle. Hence

    π(cone R)=τ_B(cone R)

for every R, where τ_B is the edge bipartization number. This is an exact elementary identity, not an imported parity theorem.

## Edge-cover-critical cones from α-critical triangle-free rims

Assume R is connected, triangle-free, has n vertices and independence number α, and deleting every rim edge raises α by one. (This is ordinary α-edge-criticality.) Its cone G has only triangles auv for uv∈E(R). Therefore

    ν(G)=μ(R),     τ(G)=vc(R)=n−α.

For the cover identity, replace each selected rim edge uv by one endpoint spoke; that rim edge occurs in only its own apex triangle, so no triangle becomes uncovered and the cover does not grow. Selected spokes then correspond to an ordinary vertex cover of R. Packing triangles correspond exactly to matching rim edges.

Deleting rim edge e gives τ(G−e)=n−α(R−e)=τ(G)−1. Every spoke is critical too. For v∈V(R), choose an incident edge vu. An independent set of size α+1 in R−vu must contain both v,u; deleting v gives a maximum R-independent set that avoids v. Thus α(R−v)=α(R), and

    τ(G−av)=vc(R−v)=n−1−α=τ(G)−1.

The rim edges incident with v remain in G−av but lie in no triangle and do not change this identity. Thus EVERY graph edge is cover-critical.

Because R is connected and nonempty, the apex triangles form one shared-edge component: triangles based on incident rim edges share a spoke, and the line graph of connected R is connected.

## Quantitative odd-cover lower bound

For every induced rim subgraph on q vertices, its independence number is at most α. The elementary random-order argument gives an independent set of expected size Σ_v1/(d(v)+1)≥q²/(2e+q); consequently

    e≥q(q−α)/(2α).

For completeness, selecting the vertices earlier than all their neighbors in a uniformly random vertex ordering gives an independent set and the stated expectation; Cauchy–Schwarz gives the final inequality.

For a cut of the cone, let its rim parts have sizes p,q with p+q=n and q≤p. The apex contributes at least q monochromatic spokes. The rim contributes at least

    p(p−α)/(2α)+q(q−α)/(2α).

The sum is a convex quadratic in q, minimized over the real interval[0,n/2] at q=(n−α)/2, and has minimum

    (n²−α²)/(4α).

Thus

    π(G)≥(n²−α²)/(4α),     2ν(G)=2μ(R)≤n.

In particular α/n<sqrt(5)−2 makes π(G)>2ν(G). The ordinary Tuza inequality nevertheless holds: the endpoints of a maximum ordinary matching form a vertex cover of R, so τ(G)=vc(R)≤2μ(R)=2ν(G).

## Such critical rims exist with arbitrarily small independence ratio

Fix ρ>0 small. On N vertices choose each ordinary graph edge independently with probability p=N^(−3/4). The expected number of triangles is at most N^(3/4)/6, so by Markov the probability of more than N/2 triangles tends to zero. For k=floor(ρN), the probability of an independent k-set is at most

    2^N exp(−p binom(k,2)) →0.

For sufficiently large N choose a graph with at most N/2 triangles and α<k≤ρN. Delete one vertex from each triangle, taking the union. At least N/2 vertices remain, no triangle remains, and the independence ratio is at most2ρ.

Now repeatedly delete an edge whenever doing so preserves α. The process terminates, preserves triangle-freeness and α, and leaves every retained edge α-critical. Deleting a single edge can raise α by at most one, so every such increase is exactly one. Independence numbers add over connected components. Therefore some component has independence ratio at most2ρ; choosing 2ρ<1 ensures that component is nontrivial rather than isolated. It is connected, triangle-free and α-edge-critical.

Letting ρ tend to zero makes the lower-bound ratio π(G)/(2ν(G)) at least(1−δ²)/(4δ), where δ=α/n, arbitrarily large. This is an all-size ordinary existence proof, independent of Ramsey catalogues.

## Why these cones fail two-edge resilience

Choose ANY rim edge uv. A matching in R−{u,v} can be adjoined to uv, so μ(R−{u,v})≤μ(R)−1. Deleting the two apex spokes au,av leaves exactly the apex triangles based in R−{u,v}; consequently ν(G−{au,av})≤ν(G)−1. This elementary augmentation proves the all-size failure directly. No Tutte–Berge or other matching theorem is needed. The previously frozen TUZA-17 notes/A_CRITICAL_NONSATURATION.md used Tutte–Berge for a more cumbersome proof of existence of such a one-or-two-vertex blocker; the present observation removes that unnecessary dependency, including for its NS/NS-state deduction.

Thus none of these examples is a counterexample to H-O2, and no property of an actual minimal Tuza violation is asserted merely from criticality alone.

## Compact literal witness: the Kalbfleisch cone

Take R on Z/35Z, joining vertices at circular distances1,7,11,16. This is the classical Kalbfleisch1966 Ramsey graph; the precise circulant description was read from Goedgebeur–Radziszowski's primary2012 slides, printed page15:
https://www.cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/ber12.pdf . Their paper is arXiv:1210.5826v4, Electron. J. Combin.20(1)(2013). Its uniqueness/census theorem is NOT needed by the verification below.

The local script checks/global_routes_parity_cone.py reconstructs the140 rim edges and verifies no actual rim triangle. It enumerates all73,926 independent sets, obtaining counts by size

    1,35,455,2905,10150,20265,22995,13760,3360,0

for sizes0 through9. Hence α(R)=8. For each of the four circular-distance edge orbits, it retains an independent9-set after deleting representative edge{0,d}; cyclic translations cover every rim edge, proving α-criticality directly. A size8 set avoiding0 supplies the spoke-criticality witness.

The full cone has36 vertices,175 edges and exactly140 actual triangles. It has

    ν=17,     τ=27,     π≥ceil((35²−8²)/32)=37>34=2ν.

The17 packed triangles are a(0)(1), a(2)(3), …, a(32)(33). The upper bound17 follows because every packed triangle uses two distinct spokes. A minimum27-cover uses the spokes outside one displayed independent8-set; the α computation proves optimality.
