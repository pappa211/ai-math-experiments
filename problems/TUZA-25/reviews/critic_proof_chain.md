## What proper-subgraph CS actually supplies

Assuming a minimum-edge CS failure G exists, CS is available on every proper edge-subgraph of that fixed G. The archived actual-residual profile is an explicit input at its stated scope; it is not re-proved or newly formalized in this review.

CS on a smaller graph gives nonnegative defect. At defect zero it additionally identifies B, meaning literal K4/K5 support on each nonempty shared-edge triangle component. **It does not classify a graph of defect one.** Such a graph is strict and entirely compatible with CS. The accepted local proofs use CS only for these nonnegativity and tightness consequences, except where an independent minimum choice is expressly invoked.

All graphs to which this proper-CS premise is applied in the integrated chain are edge-subgraphs of the fixed G with fewer edges. No same-edge-count vertex split or arbitrary new graph outside G is silently treated as a proper subgraph. Clique support components and normalized support graphs also remain edge-subgraphs of G.

## The separate minimum Q is legitimate

The actual triangle residual's support S lies outside C12 by the cited all-size strict-restoration theorems, including their arbitrary-background scope. Hence the collection of connected, nonempty, one-edge-resilient, defect-one support graphs inside proper edge-subgraphs of the fixed finite G and outside C12 is nonempty. It is finite after support normalization, so a minimum supported-edge count exists.

Choosing Q from this collection does not assume the desired named classification. It is a minimum *nonnamed* state within a precisely specified finite universe. A smaller support with all the same membership predicates must be named by this choice. This is independent of proper-subgraph CS and must remain described that way.

The choice does not preserve the original H's every-triangle-extension property, its exact missing-triangle parent, the parent's two-edge resilience, its original restoration boundary, or membership in the broader frozen F. The revised chain explicitly declines those inferences. It may use a theorem on Q only when that theorem's hypotheses belong to this wider class or are separately verified for the local configuration.

## Separator argument at Q

The exact one-edge transfer supplies a smaller active side that is connected, resilient and defect one. Its edge set is strictly smaller than Q, so minimum-Q comparison is legitimate. If this active side is nonnamed, minimality is contradicted directly. If it is W5 or K5-e, its edge eligibility prevents the required neutral attachment. If it is a named fresh-edge support, reattaching the actual fresh clique leaf yields another fresh-edge defect-one support, named by the archived construction-class classification. The two-tight-sides case is itself a named fresh-edge graph.

This reverse closure is necessary: merely discovering a named smaller side would not otherwise contradict Q. The chain now states it. It follows that Q has no two-vertex separator, hence is three-vertex-connected. It does not follow that Q has a degree-three vertex, a clique leaf, or any other reduction configuration.

## Critical-triangle branch

The leaf predecessor J must have the explicitly verified properties: defect two, K4-completion of every actual J triangle, a wholly critical separator triangle, and connected triangle support. K4-completion is not a consequence of proper-CS or of Q's wider minimum choice. Even K4-completion of a larger graph need not survive deletion of an arbitrary leaf, so the predecessor hypothesis must not be omitted.

For a genuine triangle-overlap leaf, connected old support follows because all new triangles attach to the single old component containing the separator triangle; they cannot join distinct old components. The critical-edge theorem then gives R=J-e with connected support, defect one and one-edge resilience. Its support is a strictly smaller edge-subgraph of Q and therefore of G. These are exactly the predicates required by the chosen minimum-Q class. No triangle-extension or actual-parent inheritance is needed for that comparison.

Minimum Q therefore makes this R support named. Diamond confinement reduces all its possible restored e-pages to the finite support insertion domain, including arbitrary background. The complete independent 210-case check then contradicts J's wholly critical triangle. This soundly excludes the *specified local configuration* in Q. It does not prove the configuration exists. Nor does it assert that arbitrary edge deletion preserves membership in frozen F.

## Matching branch and marked boundary

The revised zero-gain argument is valid without triangle extension: two new triangles on opposite K4 bases share only the fresh vertex and append to every packing avoiding that matching. This forces packing loss two. The minimum-cover argument supplies a matching with cover loss two when the predecessor has the explicitly stated defect three. The revised proof chain retains that defect-three premise instead of treating zero gain as a cover calculation.

The three-edge restoration lemma then proves resilience of R=J-M under K4-completion and proper-CS. Component additivity gives one smaller connected resilient defect-one component C and tight B components. Minimum Q may classify C as named when this whole construction lies inside Q. Proper-CS alone may not do so. The full R need not have connected support, and C is not automatically an actual minimum-CS triangle residual.

The newly proved boundary confinement preserves all outside triangles, shared vertices and background. Its exact response is nu(H)=r+nu(K) and tau(H)=2r+tau(K-F). The marked-edge term is essential; substituting tau(K) or assuming simultaneous eligibility would lose information. Resilience descends to K by exact deletion-by-deletion packing additivity. If a tight component is removed, supported-edge count strictly decreases. If none exists, K=H and no such descent occurs.

CS(K) leaves defect zero or one. At defect zero it gives B; at defect one it does not give a classification. A minimum-Q comparison would additionally require that K be strictly smaller and satisfy the whole wider-class profile. Even when its active support is named, the removed tight pieces may share extra vertices and have nontrivial background connections. Their one-edge triangle boundaries are not automatically fresh graph gluings. The chain correctly leaves the reconstruction through marked F and these retained pieces open.

## Preserved and unpreserved interfaces

- A support separator peel preserves connected support, one-edge resilience and defect one; exact packing additivity also preserves triangle extension if it was present initially.
- The defect-two critical-edge deletion preserves connected support under its separate connectivity hypothesis, as well as one-edge resilience and defect one. It does not prove triangle extension, three-connectivity, two-edge resilience, or a specific missing-triangle parent.
- Matching deletion preserves one-edge resilience and defect one under its exact numerical and K4-completion premises. It may leave several triangle components. Selecting the active component loses attachment and parent data unless that data is separately retained.
- Tight-component boundary removal preserves current outside triangles and all vertices/background, with an explicit marked-cover response. It does not assert freshness of vertex overlaps or close the reconstruction.

The integrated chain respects these boundaries. The obsolete draft restriction requiring triangle extension for the local opposite-pair loss was removed by a valid direct packing argument; it must not reappear as either an unproved inheritance claim or an unnecessary obstruction to applying the revised local theorem.

## Exact stopping point

The chain has checked local reductions with decreasing measures, a complete finite named-interface exclusion, and an explicit proper active-region response. It lacks a theorem that every unknown Q contains an applicable configuration, and it lacks a complete reconstruction/exclusion for the surviving marked matching interface. Finite searches reaching no difficult states cannot fill either gap.

Accordingly this is a noncircular partial route and a concrete set of remaining obligations. It is not a closed well-founded induction, an unrestricted CS proof, an unrestricted Tuza proof, or a classification of all defect-one supports. This conclusion is independent AI review of the supplied chain, not external peer review or a new Lean audit.
