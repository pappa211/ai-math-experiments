### Local lemmas, proved without a finite checker (138-140)

For K4 the minimum cover is an opposite-edge pair. Every face then has one red. For any forbidden edge, the two faces avoiding it have union five edges, so a cover with at most four reds cannot make both all-red. A non-all-red face is eligible because the restriction is a cover. This proves arbitrary-root avoidance through local excess two.

For K5, a minimum cover has size four and its blue complement is K2,3. An elementary justification: a triangle-free five-vertex graph with six edges has maximum degree three; its three neighbors are independent and the remaining vertex must join all three. Maximum degree four permits only four edges, and maximum degree at most two permits at most five. Therefore every minimum cover is, up to labels, red ab together with red cd,ce,de. One-red two-packings avoiding representative red or blue roots are:

- avoid ab: acd,bce;
- avoid cd: ace,bde;
- avoid ac: ade,bce.

These root orbits cover every edge. Adding one red to a minimum cover cannot turn a one-red triangle all-red. If a five-red cover contains no minimum four-cover, it is inclusion-minimal and its five-blue-edge complement is maximal triangle-free. A maximal bipartite triangle-free graph is complete bipartite; on five vertices those complete bipartite edge counts are four or six. A nonbipartite triangle-free graph on five vertices contains C5 and has no chord. Hence the five-blue graph is C5 and there is no all-red triangle either (the complementary red graph is also C5). All triangles are then eligible, and a two-packing avoiding any edge exists by deleting that edge from K5. Thus K5 covers of size <=5 have full eligible packings avoiding any specified edge.

For K5 covers of size <=6 and a specified **red** forbidden edge e, let F be eligible triangles avoiding e and suppose no two are disjoint. Every blue edge f is in at least two members of F: K5 has three triangles through f, and at most one also contains the distinct e. Every such triangle is eligible because it has a blue edge and the restriction is a cover. In a common-edge intersecting family, those two triangles force the common edge to equal f, impossible for at least four blue edges. In the K4-face alternative, let z be the fifth vertex. No blue edge can touch z, as it would belong to F outside that K4. For every blue edge f inside the K4, the triangle f+z must contain e; consequently e=zw and all blue f are incident to w. There are at most three such edges, again contradicting at least four. The red-root lemma follows.

A full eligible two-packing without a forbidden root also survives any two extra K5 reds: choose two blue edges. If adjacent ab,ac, use abd,ace with the two remaining vertices d,e; if disjoint ab,cd, use abz,cdz with the fifth vertex z. The chosen triangles are edge-disjoint and have a blue edge; the cover property supplies at least one red each.

The new vertex sets are disjoint, and every new vertex has no neighbor outside that one clique. Write d4,d5 for the numbers, b=d4+2d5, and R=(V(H),E(H)\D). R deletes the ROOT edge, rather than merely the fresh edges. The block supports and E(R) partition E(G). Every actual triangle is either a core triangle or belongs to one attachment; triangles containing a new vertex have all three vertices in its clique. The core and local descriptions overlap only at the root edge, never at an entire triangle.

A K4 has cover number 2, packing number 1, and its minimum covers are its three opposite-edge pairs. Every face then has exactly one red edge. Two of its faces avoid any prescribed root. A K5 has cover number 4 and packing number 2. Indeed, the blue complement of a four-cover is a triangle-free six-edge graph, necessarily K2,3: a degree-three vertex has three independent neighbors; the fifth vertex must join all three, while degree four is incompatible with six edges. Conversely these partitions give four-covers. With the blue bipartition {a,b}|{c,d,e}, the red edges are ab,cd,ce,de. The one-red disjoint pairs acd,bce; ace,bde; ade,bce avoid roots ab,cd,ac respectively. These are the three root-edge orbits, so a minimum-colored K5 has a two-packing avoiding ANY prescribed actual root. Any root belongs to some minimum local cover by putting its endpoints in one side of a 2|3 partition.

For every cover of G the disjoint supports force |C|≥2d4+4d5+τ(R)=2b+τ(R). Choose a minimum A on R; in each attachment choose a minimum local cover containing its root. This covers every core triangle because it either contains a root or is a triangle of R, and covers every attachment. Thus τ(G)=2b+τ(R). ### Conditional cut clauses

Fix a prescribed actual edge-disjoint triangle packing P, minimum C, and deletion set S⊆C. Hypotheses: no actual all-red triangle is contained in S, and S meets every all-red member of P. Variables x_b range over all original blue actual edges and indicate added B. These hypotheses ensure a packed all-red triangle loses one or two reds, so becomes eligible, and no all-red triangle loses coverage.

Coverage requires exactly: (i) x_b=1 for the sole blue of every T2 with both reds in S; (ii) x_b∨x_c for every T1 whose sole red lies in S. Packed eligibility additionally requires exactly: (iii) x_b=0 for a packed T2 with both reds retained; (iv) ¬x_b∨¬x_c for a packed T1 with its red retained. Other cases impose no extra condition. In particular a packed T2 losing one red stays eligible regardless of x_b; a packed T1 losing its red needs coverage but selecting both blues gives two reds, not all-red. These clauses plus the hypotheses characterize cover preservation and eligibility for every P member.

Let the combined undirected graph of BOTH positive and negative pairs be bipartite L|R; forced units create no pair edges. Put y_l=x_l, y_r=1−x_r. A positive pair becomes implication y_r⇒y_l. A negative pair becomes y_l⇒y_r. Add unit arcs l→t and s→r; add implication arcs with M=N_blue+1 and force y=1 by s→v of capacity M and y=0 by v→t of capacity M.
