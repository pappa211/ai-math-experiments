## Equivalent formulations and non-implications

Tripartiteness of D(H) is exactly a three-label coloring of graph edges with rainbow triangles; this is called triangle-3-colorability. It is equivalent to three-colorability of the anti-Gallai graph of H. That anti-Gallai graph must be recomputed after deletion: simply removing three vertices from the original anti-Gallai graph can retain false adjacencies supported by destroyed triangles.

Four-colorability of H implies the hypothesis, but is strictly stronger. The saved Mycielski sanity example has H not four-colorable and D(H) tripartite. K5 itself meets the triangle-deletion hypothesis, since K5−E(T) is three-colorable, although K5 is neither four-colorable nor triangle-3-colorable. K6 has OCT four and its triangle-deleted remainder is four-colorable. Thus results assuming these properties of G itself do not settle the full claims.

The triangle graph is a different construction, with triangles as vertices. K5's triangle graph is L(K5), containing an induced five-cycle; the perfect-triangle-graph theorem does not subsume the deletion claim. K6 contains an odd five-wheel as a subgraph. Complete tripartite graphs K_(2,m,m) have OCT at most two and unbounded degree, maximum average degree, treewidth, and packing number, ruling out automatic containment in bounded-parameter theorems.

The abstract hypergraph version obtained by deleting the vertices of one hyperedge is false: in the Fano plane it leaves no edges, while tau=3 and nu=1. Graphical realizability is indispensable. The original proof and Lean statement retain it.

## Current and adjacent literature

The independent review examines Szestopalow's 2016 dissertation (home-base Theorem 3.1.2, p. 20; ordinary tripartite-graph Theorem 4.1.5, p. 73), Munaro's 2016 dissertation §3.3, and Gutiérrez Alva's 2020 dissertation as citation trails. Relevant cited and citing results were followed; this was not an exhaustive reading of every indexed item.

Recent primary records checked include Yi arXiv2608.23010v1 (factor 63/22), Wang 2609.13831v1 (factor 165/59), Kandasamy 2610.15269v1 (maximum degree≤10), Huang et al. *Épi* 2610.24944v1 (specified split K8 graphs with at most three active neighborhood types), and Zijian Zeng Preprints.org202608.1304v1 (at most two types). None states the three targets or supplies an implication covering their full classes. Their computer-assisted certificates were not rerun. Bennett et al. 2606.09736v1 concerns random geometric graphs, not every graph satisfying the deterministic edge-count condition. These are historical version observations; the fresh publication check is recorded separately.

Two adjacent conjectures must not be treated as established or still-open shortcuts. Guruswami–Sandeep, *Approximate Hypergraph Vertex Cover and generalized Tuza's conjecture*, [ECCC TR20-167](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2020/167/download), Theorem 3, disproves the tent-free generalization with ratios approaching three. This does not refute the graphical or blown-up-hypergraph statements. Clow–Haxell–Mohar, *A Counterexample to a Conjecture of Lovász*, [arXiv2505.05339v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05339), Theorem 1.3, disproves a general two-vertex matching-reducing deletion principle for tripartite 3-graphs. It does not contradict the extremal HNS structure.
