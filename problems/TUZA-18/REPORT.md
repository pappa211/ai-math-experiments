## Strongest positive result: original-packing support with private K4s

Let P be any specified maximum triangle packing in an arbitrary finite simple G. Write B for its edge union. A triangle outside P is private for T in P if it meets B in exactly one edge of T. A private K4 completion is an actual K4 containing T whose other three faces are all private for T. Let A(P) be B together with the edges of all such completions.

**Ordinary theorem. If all members of P share one graph vertex, G has a triangle cover C contained in the ORIGINAL A(P), with |C|<=2|P|.** The common vertex need not be universal. Unmatched neighbors and vertices outside its neighborhood are allowed. The argument never substitutes a different maximum packing when defining the allowed edges.

The proof's decisive step is simultaneous replacement of two packed triangles by two old private pages. Whenever those pages are edge-disjoint, any unused edge between their two opposite matched pairs would supply a third triangle and enlarge the packing. This classifies every interpair edge matrix between active matched pairs, meaning pairs that support an old triangle: it is empty, a directed two-edge star with a unique old tip, or a rigid P4/C4 pair block. The resulting directed graph has outdegree at most one. A reverse leaf exchange constructs an independent halfset: either add one leaf endpoint, or remove its selected parent center and add both leaf endpoints. Cyclic and rigid terminal states have explicit independent sets.

An active matched pair with an unmatched neighbor forces an ORIGINAL private K4. Its new allowed spoke costs one edge, paid by starting with both endpoints at that pair's sink component. Combining the independent set, all active bases and the remaining spokes covers every old and apex triangle within budget. The separate local corollary needs only stability against 0→1, 1→2 and 2→3 augmentations; a locally stable star need not be globally maximum. Such an augmentation can destroy the common-vertex property, so this is not an iterative unrestricted algorithm.

Proof: [common-vertex theorem](notes/criticality_star_pk_theorem.md), SHA-256 `f08cc442241eef4b790bc4b5228c958045593f97ebf56c1259a6f6b1ffe5488b`; [local corollary](notes/criticality_star_local_dichotomy.md). Those bounded checks support the audit; the ordinary proof establishes the all-size statement.

## Strongest late obstruction: connected chains defeat bounded strict descent

The star theorem suggests assigning a center to each packed triangle, applying it within each star group, and choosing local covers that also hit triangles crossing groups. That simultaneous selection is the global SG hypothesis, not a consequence of the local theorem.

For a SUPPORT-CONTAINED cover C, call a packed triangle heavy, balanced or light when it contains respectively three, two or one selected edges. A cover admits a partition into common-center groups, each costing at most twice its packing size, exactly when heavy triangles can be matched injectively to intersecting light triangles. In the pure-type 1 setting all covers in A(P) are support-contained, so this gives a precise structural subgate.

A seven-vertex witness refutes both canonical opposite-base centers and the assertion that every minimum support cover works. It has nine minimum support covers of size 8: one fails Hall compatibility and eight succeed. Its UNRESTRICTED cover number is 5, so minimum-global-cover exchange results cannot be imported for these support minima. The [literal witness and equivalence](notes/late_star_gluing_obstruction.md) were independently reconstructed.

The next proposal required one equal-cost support-edge pivot to strictly lower Hall deficiency whenever it was positive. Initial fixed-corpus tests found no failure. A vertex-glued chain then defeated it, but separate triangle components explained that obstruction. Fifty two-block outside-support edge gluings all admitted the desired repair. Their exact interface structure led to the decisive unbounded construction.

**Ordinary obstruction theorem. For every N>=2 there is a graph with 5N+2 vertices, 16N+1 edges, 16N actual triangles, and ONE shared-edge triangle component, together with an all-type 1 maximum packing P of size 4N and a minimum B-contained cover C of size 8N having Hall deficiency 1. Any compatible minimum B-cover has symmetric difference at least 2 ceil(N/2) from C, equivalently it replaces at least ceil(N/2) selected edges. Explicit simultaneous pivots attain that distance.** Thus for every fixed radius r, some such graph has no strict deficiency improvement replacing at most r selected edges.

The construction glues copies of the seven-vertex block along outside-support edges. All triangles retain unique block ownership, so maximum packing and support-cover minima factor exactly. Every minimum cover has equal heavy/light counts inside each block. Across a prefix cut, a saturating matching must therefore cross equally in both directions. Two adjacent unchanged blocks offer only one direction, forcing changed blocks to contain the first block and meet every path edge. This gives the lower bound; odd-block light pivots attain it.

The [full proof](notes/late_star_connected_pivot_obstruction.md), SHA-256 `0a5491534d7c85fb7206e4f0ffddafaf1c179f2b40ad0c198135f229e124f4cd`, and [independent continuation review](review/chain_review.md) agree. Separate N=3 checking reconstructed all 48 triangles, nu=12, support minimum 24, all 729 minimum-cover products, and exact repair distance 4. This refutes bounded STRICT descent of that potential, including its single-triangle-component repair. It does not refute plateau moves, richer separator states, changing P, existential SG, PK or Tuza. The examples explicitly admit compatible covers and satisfy Tuza.

## Earlier global mechanisms and the evidence-driven repairs

**Packing-guided cuts.** The initial rule selects every noncrossing packed edge and each outside-support edge that is uniquely noncrossing in a triangle. It always covers, but cones over triangle-free small-independence rims force cost>2nu for EVERY maximum packing and EVERY cut. Allowing deletion of selected packed edges repaired that cone failure, but K4 incidence blocks whose balancing constraints are inconsistent defeated the repair. A fully retained 155-vertex graph has nu=155, tau=310, and rule cost at least 311. Allowing a separate cut in each triangle component repaired that example. A robust K4-block construction with one universal apex then defeated the connected repair, even at minimum triangle codegree 3, while a separate matching-based cover proves Tuza for the entire family. These are exact architecture failures, not claims that arbitrary cut methods fail. See [initial cut](notes/global_cut_gate.md), [pruning](notes/apex_pruned_critique.md), and [connected obstruction](notes/criticality_connected_pc.md).

**Finite incidence abstraction.** For every fixed q and epsilon>0 in the stated range, a self-contained probabilistic argument gives a 3-uniform abstract hypergraph with tau>(3−epsilon)nu whose every induced patch on at most q hypervertices is the FULL triangle hypergraph of a simple graph. Those representations need not agree globally. Extracting a hypervertex-minimal violation preserves the patches and all listed generic criticality arithmetic, including one/two-deletion resilience, prescribed one/two-packing extension and tight triangle deletion. Thus finitely many bounded incidence exclusions plus that arithmetic cannot supply the proposed enlarged-class theorem. Global endpoints, unbounded neighborhoods and graph-realizable iterative operations remain possible. See [incidence proof](notes/global_finite_incidence_gate.md), [criticality strengthening](notes/criticality_global_gate.md), and the separate [source-access correction](evidence/source_access_correction.md). These are NOT graph counterexamples.

**Apex cover restriction.** A literal seven-vertex graph has true tau=nu=3, while extending only minimum old covers costs 4. Joining k copies at the apex gives a loss k. The exact unrestricted extension identity must retain the full forced-edge penalty, not merely whether an edge set lies in an old minimum cover. This does NOT refute the weaker 2nu budget compatibility tested in the prior apex framework. That bound survived the specified finite tests here and remains unproved. The old TUZA-17 budget-seven/Ramsey-catalogue boundary was not promoted to an independently verified global input. See [proof and amplified witness](notes/apex_minimum_restriction.md).

**Criticality shortcuts.** K5 and two edge-glued K5s defeat small-packing-blocker conclusions from cover-criticality, even with odd tau. K2 joined to C5 defeats clique/codegree conclusions from the property that every triangle belongs to a minimum cover; its two-edge resilience fails. Another control distinguishes resilience from that cover property. Fixed-size packing-extension guarantees and large codegree do not automatically extend to all packing sizes. None of these relaxed controls is represented as an actual minimal Tuza violation. The exact controls and unresolved conjunctions are in [the criticality handoff](notes/criticality_track_status.md).

**PK substeps.** A six-vertex graph defeats universal private-tip/base role separation for EVERY maximum packing. An all-type 1 construction embeds arbitrary core triangle geometry; a K6 core refutes canonical multicover splitting without refuting the one-cover budget. Uniform fixed coefficients below two for any private-page type are impossible, even with strategic maximum packing choice. Dropping maximum cardinality from the type 0 claim fails on 48 vertices: the prescribed 256 packing needs at least 528 support-cover edges, but a 260 packing exposes the missing hypothesis and a 376 ordinary cover certifies Tuza. A proposed product repair has a 5-for-4 augmentation. General type 0 and type 1 PK remain unresolved. **Triangle-preserving graph coverings and vertex identification.** Genuine locally bijective covers in which every triangle closes split any cone into unchanged sheets. A cone over Mycielski(C5), independently checked with nu=5 and tau=6, therefore defeats the specified cover-to-rainbow/K5-component existence route. Arbitrary pair identification with nondecreasing defect tau−2nu fails on cone C5. Enlarging its terminal family by ALL common-vertex maximum-packing graphs still fails on C7 squared: it has nu=3,tau=4, largest star size 2, while all 21 quotients have nu=tau=3. These exact routes were closed; positive-defect-only variants were not disproved or supplied. **Degree-directed edge compression.** The final route moves a lower-degree vertex's exclusive neighbor edges to a higher-degree receiver, preserving vertex and edge counts. The sum of squared degrees strictly increases, so iteration terminates at threshold graphs. Using the published [threshold-graph terminal theorem](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/7660/download), a universally available move that never lowers d=tau−2nu would prove Tuza. The availability assertion fails on two edge-glued K4s. Adding the entire common-vertex maximum-packing class to the terminals still fails on the 4-by-4 rook graph. Closing those terminal classes under triangle-component decomposition is defeated by the rook graph's cone: it has one triangle component, exact nu=16,tau=24,d=−8, and no common-vertex maximum packing. Every nontrivial permitted move belongs to one of two symmetry orbits. Explicit compressed packings and covers give d<=−9 and d<=−12 respectively, with all newly created triangles included. These are certified bounds, not asserted compressed optima. The [frozen proof and certificates](notes/late_compression_gate.md) passed [independent continuation review](review/compression_review.md); the nine-file review package froze at 20:27:31 UTC. The entire previously proved one-apex block class already contains this cone; exempting that WHOLE class leaves a stronger availability proposal unresolved, without a new mechanism. No positive-defect-only claim is refuted by these controls.
