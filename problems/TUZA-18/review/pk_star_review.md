For a maximum triangle packing P, B is its graph-edge support. A private page of T in P is an actual triangle different from T whose intersection with B consists of exactly one edge of T. A(P) contains B and every edge of a K4 completing T whose three other faces are all private. PK-all requires a triangle cover of size at most 2|P| within A(P) for every maximum P; PK-exists requires one favorable maximum P. Both would imply Tuza. They remain unproved globally.

The local page dichotomy is valid. If abx and acy are private pages on distinct edges of packed abc with x different from y, their graph edges are disjoint and avoid all other packed triangles, giving a two-for-one augmentation. Thus page tips on distinct edges must be the same unique vertex x. Graph closure supplies all three spokes ax,bx,cx outside B, so the third K4 face is private as well. This proves the stated private-K4/book/empty trichotomy; it does not resolve interactions between distinct packed triangles.

**The scoped one-apex K4-block PK-all proof survives independent reconstruction.** Every packing's apex bases form a matching. A block containing zero or one such base must also contain an old packed face, since a suitable face could otherwise be appended. Two bases in one block are opposite and meet every old face. Normalizing every two-base block leaves one old face per block and an apex matching with at most one base per block.

That matching is inclusion-maximal under the block restriction: an appendable base in an unused block can be accommodated by changing its old face to avoid the base, while a background base meets no old triangle. The opposite-pair block covers plus spokes at matched endpoints therefore cover all actual triangles by the ordinary one-apex argument.

The essential new assertion, membership in the ORIGINAL A(P), is correct. An originally unused block has exactly one packed face and no other packed block edge, so all its other faces are private and its entire K4 lies in A(P). In an originally one-base block, the old packed face avoiding that base contains its opposite edge; hence both edges of the required opposite pair were already in B. ## A K4-free star-packing barrier to counterexample synthesis

The following theorem was reconstructed while trying to refute PK using a high-chromatic cone with added private rim pages. It explains why that natural attack fails. It is not asserted to provide a globally available packing in an arbitrary graph.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite simple K4-free graph and let P be a maximum edge-disjoint triangle packing such that every member contains a fixed graph vertex v. Put k=|P|. Then G has a triangle cover contained in B=E(P), of size at most 2k. More precisely, the cover below has size 2k-s, where s is the number of packed rim edges supporting an actual triangle avoiding v.

If k=0, G is triangle-free and the empty cover suffices. Assume k>0. Write each packed triangle as vab, and let M be the corresponding matching of rim edges ab. Let U be the set of its 2k endpoints. All these endpoints lie in N(v), and

    B = M union {vu : u in U}.

Let S be the subset of M consisting of edges that belong to some triangle avoiding v, and put W=V(S), so |W|=2s. The proposed cover is

    C = S union {vu : u in U minus W}.

It is contained in B and has exactly s+(2k-2s)=2k-s edges.

First, any actual triangle Q avoiding v must meet B by maximality of P. Its intersection with B can contain only rim edges from the matching M, and a triangle cannot contain two vertex-disjoint edges. Therefore Q contains exactly one edge e in M, which by definition lies in S. All triangles avoiding v are covered by C.

For every e=ab in S, choose one triangle Q_e=abx_e avoiding v. K4-freeness implies x_e is not adjacent to v, since otherwise {v,a,b,x_e} would be an actual K4. Thus every chosen tip is outside N(v), whereas every M endpoint lies in N(v). The chosen Q_e are pairwise edge-disjoint: their base edges are disjoint, and a shared outside edge would require a common base endpoint or a tip equal to another base endpoint, both impossible. Sharing just a tip does not share an edge. Each Q_e avoids the support of every packed triangle except its own base e.

Replace all packed triangles based in S by the Q_e. This is still a k-packing. If G[W] contained an edge xy not in M, then vxy would be edge-disjoint from this replacement packing: its spokes belong to removed packed triangles, and its base joins two vertices of N(v), so it is neither an outside edge of any Q_e nor a retained matching base. It could be appended, contradicting maximality. Consequently

    E(G[W]) is a subset of S.

It remains to cover an apex triangle vxy. If x or y belongs to U minus W, its selected spoke covers it. Otherwise x,y lie in W union (N(v) minus U). If both lie outside U, the triangle avoids B entirely, contradicting maximality of P. If both lie in W, the preceding induced-edge conclusion makes xy an edge of S, so it is covered.

In the remaining case, say x lies in W and y lies in N(v) minus U. Let xx' be the S edge containing x, and let Q=xx'z be its chosen old triangle, with z outside N(v). The triangle vxy is private on the packed spoke vx: vy is not in B, and xy cannot be a matching rim edge because y is unmatched. The old Q is private on the packed rim xx'. Their outside tips y and z are distinct because y is in N(v) and z is not. Directly, Q and vxy are edge-disjoint and avoid every other packed triangle. Replacing vxx' by both yields a (k+1)-packing, contradiction. This exhausts all triangles and proves the theorem.

**Scope.** No assumption that every graph triangle contains v was made. The theorem needs the specified maximum packing to have that common vertex, and no theorem supplies such a packing globally. It rules out the attempted cone/private-page counterexample construction in its K4-free form. It does not prove PK for non-star maximum packings or for graphs with K4s.

## Why removing K4-freeness changes the problem

An old page abx on a packed rim ab can have three kinds of tip. A tip outside N(v) behaves as in the theorem. A tip inside N(v) but outside U yields a private K4 completion and supplies additional allowed edges. A tip in U from another matched pair can create a K5-like internal state: its v-spoke is already in B, so it need not yield a private K4. Such internal pages can conflict with one another and cannot all be replaced simultaneously without analysis.

The K5 example already blocks a rigid one-endpoint-per-pair solution. For universal apex v and rim H=K4, choose a perfect matching M of H. All packed rim edges have old private pages, but alpha(H)=1<|M|=2. Nevertheless H-M is a four-cycle with independence number two, and the support cover can use a 3/1 allocation across packed triangles. Budget transfer is essential.

There is a useful sufficient residual condition in the no-private-K4 book case. Let F be the active matching edges supporting old triangles, W their endpoints. A book state's endpoints have no link neighbor outside U: such a neighbor would give a spoke-private page on a different packed edge and force a private K4. If

    alpha((G[N(v)]-M)[W]) >= |F|,

then selecting all F rims, a vertex cover of that residual link of size at most |F|, and all packed spokes outside W gives a support cover of size at most 2k. This is a reduction only. The required independence inequality has not been proved from global star-packing maximality.

For a universal apex, global star maximality implies another exact necessary condition: every maximum ordinary matching of H=G-v meets every old triangle, since otherwise that triangle could be appended to its apex packing. If H has a perfect matching, each old triangle must contain exactly one edge of every perfect matching; its three-vertex cut is therefore matching-tight. This identifies nontrivial matching geometry in the remaining internal-page case. No classification or general PK theorem is inferred from it.

## Additional private-tip theorem review

Write packed triangles as a_i b_i x_i with private tips x_i. Base vertices and private tips are disjoint sets. After selecting every base, any remaining actual triangle must contain at least two packed spokes: zero support edges would contradict maximality, and exactly one spoke would contradict the no-spoke-private-page hypothesis. The bipartite spoke graph rules out three spokes. Two spokes cannot share a tip from different packed triangles, and two from the same triangle close with a selected base. Therefore all remaining triangles have the exact form a x_i x_j, with bases i,j sharing anchor a. A triangle among indices in the resulting cross graph would produce an actual triangle on three private tips, avoiding the entire packing support; so that index graph is triangle-free.

The key mixed-index attack is valid. Suppose base e=ab has cross neighbors f=ac and g=bd at its two endpoints, with c different from d. The crosses U=a x_e x_f and V=b x_e x_g use distinct packed spokes and distinct tip-tip edges, so are edge-disjoint. Every selected private base page is disjoint from both crosses: privacy excludes packed spokes, and a page with two anchor endpoints contains at most one private tip, excluding tip-tip edges.

If the private pages on f and g are disjoint, those two pages and U,V replace three packed triangles by four. If the pages intersect, their disjoint bases ac and bd force reciprocal tips. Tips b for the first page or a for the second would introduce the packed central edge ab, violating privacy. The only remaining intersection pattern is acd and bcd, sharing cd. A central private page abz has z different from c and d because ac and bd are packed. Hence abz is disjoint from acd, and U,V,acd,abz give the four-for-three augmentation. This checks the difficult arbitrary-witness intersection case without assuming the page tips are external.

Thus the two neighboring bases must be ac and bc. Any additional cross neighbor at either endpoint would restore the excluded unequal-endpoint configuration, so a mixed index has exactly these two neighbors. If two mixed indices were adjacent, their forced common third neighbor would form a triangle in the index graph, contradiction. Mixed indices are consequently independent.

Selecting all bases and, at each nonmixed index, the spoke at its unique active endpoint covers every cross: every cross has a nonmixed endpoint because mixed indices are independent. Arbitrary spokes at mixed or isolated indices keep the total at most two per packed triangle. The cover lies entirely in B. The lexicographic maximum-covered-vertex corollary is also correct: if a private page has a tip outside V(P) while the opposite packed vertex occurs elsewhere in P, the one-for-one replacement preserves all old covered vertices and adds a new one. Its correct optimizer is the cardinality |V(P)| among maximum packings.

This theorem does not settle the present general-star rim-book difficulty. In a star packing, the vertex opposite a rim-private edge is the common center, which is not private among P when there is more than one packed triangle. The two scoped results address different geometries and should not be combined by silently transferring that hypothesis.
